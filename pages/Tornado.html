Cyclone Information

Learn what cyclones are, how they form, how storm surge and strong winds work, and how families, schools, and neighbours can prepare before, during, and after a cyclone.

Read Safety Tips Emergency Kit

Quick Definition

A cyclone is a large rotating storm system that forms over warm ocean water. It brings powerful winds, heavy rain, flooding, and storm surge.

Cyclones can intensify quickly — keep a warning plan ready and know your evacuation route.

About Cyclones

Cyclones are massive tropical storms that form over warm oceans. They can last for days, grow larger over time, and bring dangerous winds, flooding, and dangerous waves to coastal areas.

Warm Ocean Waters

Cyclones gather energy from warm ocean water. The warmer the water, the stronger the storm can become.

Spiral Winds

Air spirals inward around the storm centre, creating very strong winds that can damage homes, roads, and power systems.

Storm Surge

Along the coast, rising sea levels can flood streets and houses. Storm surge is one of the most dangerous parts of a cyclone.

Important Cyclone Words

These words help people understand storm updates, evacuation orders, and emergency information during a cyclone.

A cyclone watch means dangerous conditions may happen. A cyclone warning means shelter or evacuation should begin immediately.
Word Meaning
Eye The calm centre of the storm, surrounded by the strongest winds.
Eye Wall The ring of the most intense winds and heavy rain around the eye.
Cyclone Watch Cyclone conditions are possible, so people should stay alert.
Cyclone Warning A cyclone is expected or happening, so people should act quickly.
Storm Surge Sea water pushed inland by powerful winds, causing serious flooding.

How Cyclones Happen

1. Warm Ocean Fuels It

Warm ocean water gives the storm energy and helps it grow stronger.

2. Air Starts Spinning

Rising air and wind patterns create a spinning storm system.

3. The Eye Forms

A calm centre forms while very strong winds circle around it.

4. Landfall Brings Damage

When the cyclone reaches land, wind, rain, flooding, and storm surge cause severe impacts.

Safer Everyday Preparation

Higher Ground Plan

Know which roads, shelters, and safe places to go if flooding or storm surge is expected in your area.

Better Communication

Keep local radio, phone alerts, and family contact lists ready so everyone receives updates quickly.

Reusable Supplies

Prepare reusable water bottles, rechargeable lights, power banks, and medical items to stay ready during long power cuts.

Cyclone Strength

Cyclone strength is often shown with categories. Higher categories mean much stronger winds, larger storm size, and greater flooding risk.

Category Possible Damage
1 Minor damage, such as loose roof sheets and small tree branches falling.
2 Moderate damage, including roof damage, fallen trees, and local flooding.
3 Major damage with strong winds, flooding, and power outages.
4 or 5 Severe to extreme damage, including major building destruction and dangerous surge flooding.

Safety Tips

Before a Cyclone

  • Prepare an emergency kit with water, food, medications, and chargers.
  • Know if your home is in a flood or storm surge area.
  • Fill fuel, charge devices, and move valuables to higher places.
  • Listen to official weather alerts and evacuation orders.

During a Cyclone

  • Stay indoors in a strong building or safe shelter.
  • Move away from windows, doors, and low-lying areas.
  • Stay away from flooded roads, fallen wires, and unsafe coastal areas.
  • Do not go outside to watch the storm or take photos.

After a Cyclone

  • Check yourself and others for injuries and seek help if needed.
  • Avoid damaged buildings, contaminated water, and fallen power lines.
  • Use messages instead of calls if phone networks are busy.
  • Wait for official updates before returning to flooded or damaged areas.

Community Safety

  • Help neighbours who may need transport, medication, or alerts.
  • Keep shelters clear, easy to enter, and ready for evacuation.
  • Share useful information with schools, clinics, and local groups.
  • Keep roads clear for emergency workers and rescue teams.

Move Up, Stay Inside, Protect Your Head

1

Move Up

Move to higher ground or to a safer indoor level if flooding or surge is expected.

2

Stay Inside

Stay in a strong shelter until the storm passes and officials say it is safe.

3

Protect Your Head

Cover your head and neck with your arms, a bag, blanket, or pillow.

Common Cyclone Dangers

Storm Surge

Rising sea levels can flood beaches, roads, and homes. Move to higher ground early when alerts are issued.

Flooding

Heavy rain can overflow rivers, block roads, and damage buildings. Stay away from flooded areas and unstable ground.

Power Lines

Fallen wires and damaged equipment can be dangerous after a cyclone. Never touch them or walk through standing water near them.
